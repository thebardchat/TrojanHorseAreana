## R-004.1 — Bid law thresholds for school work in Alabama

QUESTION: Above what dollar amounts must a county board of education competitively bid construction work, and other purchases?

ANSWER (one paragraph): **Construction ("public works"): sealed bids above $100,000. Other purchases by a school board (Title 16 competitive bid law): $40,000 or more.**
- Ala. Code §39-2-2(a)(1): "Before entering into any contract for a public works involving an amount in excess of one hundred thousand dollars ($100,000), the awarding authority shall advertise for sealed bids."
- §39-2-2(b)(1): contracts of $100,000 or less may be let "with or without advertising or sealed bids."
- §39-2-2(a)(5): a project over $100,000 may not "be split into parts" to avoid bidding.
- "Public works" (§39-2-1(6), as quoted by DCM) means work on public property "to be paid, in whole or in part, with public funds."
- 2026 change: the enrolled text of 2026 SB196 amends §39-2-2, keeps the $100,000 threshold, and says "This act shall become effective on June 1, 2026."
- DCM adds its own rule for projects in its jurisdiction (which includes K-12 schools): for public works of $100,000 or less, DCM "requires solicitation of at least three bids," documented.
- Non-construction purchases by city and county boards of education fall under Ala. Code §16-13B-1. The threshold is **$40,000** "per Act 2023-203," according to the Alabama Department of Examiners of Public Accounts (Feb 2025 manual).
- The same manual lists an AG opinion: purchases by individual schools of money raised at "athletic events, snack bars, and other non-tax sources need not comply with the Competitive Bid Law" (AG 81-00584).

## R-004.2 — Do donated labor or donated materials change the rules?

QUESTION: If labor or materials are donated (not paid with school money), do the bid law and state review still apply?

ANSWER (one paragraph): **The sources split the two questions. On bidding, Alabama AG opinions say work paid entirely with private funds is not "public works." On review and inspection, DCM and the district apply the same process whatever the funding.**
- AG opinions listed in the Examiners manual (Feb 2025):
  - "Works to be performed on public property … that are paid for entirely with private funds are not public works, and contracts to perform such works are not subject to the competitive bidding requirements of the Public Works Law" (AG 2004-026, City of Pelham).
  - "If the project will be paid for entirely with private funds, the project is not subject to the competitive bidding requirements of the Public Works Law" (AG 2004-223, City of Boaz).
  - "The donation of construction services to the Park and Recreation Authority is not subject to the Competitive Bid Law or the Public Works Law" (AG 2000-003, Tuscaloosa County Park and Recreation Authority).
- **None of these opinions involves a board of education**, and none was read in the original. They were read as summarized by the Examiners.
- The Alabama League of Municipalities adds its own view: "if private funds are disbursed by the city or through the city, they would become public funds." That is the League's opinion, not an AG holding.
- DCM: "fully locally-funded" K-12 projects expressly include "grants and donations," and DCM's jurisdiction covers K-12 work "regardless of funding source." Donations do not remove DCM review or inspection (see R-002.1).
- The MCSS Booster & Support Organizations Guidelines say:
  - "All projects should follow the same process regardless of funding."
  - Bid-law compliance "will be applicable in most situations depending on the funding sources."
  - A "Board employee should be in charge of the project."
- No source found addresses **volunteer (unpaid) labor** on a public school construction project in Alabama. The only volunteer-labor policy found was an ADECA CDBG housing policy (1995): volunteers supervised by a licensed contractor who carries liability insurance and workers' comp. It is a different program, noted for context only.

## R-004.3 — Licensing: general contractor, plumbing, electrical

QUESTION: What state licenses apply to the people doing the work (general contractor threshold, plumbing, electrical)?

ANSWER (one paragraph): **General contractor license: at $100,000 or more. Plumbing and electrical: a state license is required except listed exemptions (owner, owner's regular maintenance employee). The sources do not say whether volunteers fit any exemption.**
- **General contractor.** Ala. Code §34-8-1(a), as changed by Act 2024-277 (effective Oct 1, 2024), defines a general contractor as one who "for a fixed price, commission, fee, or wage" undertakes construction, alteration, maintenance, or repair "where the cost of the undertaking is one hundred thousand dollars ($100,000) or more."
  - The GC Board says the cost includes "labor and materials."
  - DCM: "A General Contractor's License is not required for contracts in amounts of less than $100,000.00."
- **Plumbing.** Ala. Code §34-37-6(a) makes it unlawful to "contract, engage in, offer to engage, or convey the impression that he or she is certified in plumbing" without a state certificate, "except as hereinafter provided." §34-37-15(a) lists work anyone may do without a certificate, still "subject to an inspection and approval." That includes:
  - (1) a person "regularly employed or acting as a maintenance person" doing work "on the premises of the employer"
  - (5) "Plumbing work performed by a property owner in or about a building owned or occupied by the owner"
  - The PGFB FAQ lists the same exemptions plus small items: lavatory fixture work that doesn't remove a stop valve; toilet filler/flapper/handle repair.
  - Note: the chapter defines "plumbing work" as work on pipes and fixtures "located on private property" (§34-37-1(12)). The sources do not explain how that applies on public school property.
- **Electrical.** Board of Electrical Contractors rule 303-X-3-.07 (eff. Feb 14, 2026) exempts, among others:
  - "(2) Electrical installation, service or repair work performed by an owner of a building on said building owned by him or her"
  - "(3) … anyone who is regularly employed as a maintenance person … on the premises of said owner"
  - "(8) The owner of any premises or any employee of any such owner"
- **This project's own rule** (prompt §2 / phase1.yaml W2): plumbing design is "BY LICENSED PLUMBER / ENGINEER OF RECORD" whatever the exemptions.

## R-004.4 — Bonds and insurance

QUESTION: What do the sources say about bonds and insurance for small school projects or owner-run/volunteer work?

ANSWER (one paragraph): **Bonds: not required under $100,000. Insurance: DCM tells owners who self-perform to check with their carrier. No MCSS insurance or volunteer rule was found.**
- DCM: "Performance & Payment Bonds are not required for contracts in amounts of less than $100,000.00. §39-1-1(e)." Also: "Bid Bonds are not required for contracts in amounts of less than $100,000.00" (DCM Manual Ch. 6.D.2).
- DCM self-performance page: "Owners must consult their insurance carrier and verify the insurance carried is adequate for the proposed project to cover all parties."
- When there is a construction contract, DCM's General Conditions (Form C-8, Article 37) set the contractor's insurance requirements. That article was **not read**.
- MCSS guidelines mention insurance only as an operating cost of new structures. An MCSS policy on volunteers or contractor insurance was not found (the board policy manual did not load).

## Conflicts / flags
- **Bid law vs. state review:** AG opinions (as summarized) put entirely privately funded work outside the Public Works *bid* law. DCM still claims *review and inspection* of K-12 work "regardless of funding source." The MCSS guidelines apply "the same process regardless of funding." **Donated does not mean unreviewed.**
- **Plumbing scope wording:** §34-37-1(12) speaks of plumbing "located on private property." How the PGFB chapter applies on public school property was not found. Ask PGFB or the DCM inspector.
- **GC license wording:** §34-8-1(a) covers work done "for a fixed price, commission, fee, or wage." The sources do not address unpaid volunteers. Not interpreted here.

## What this means for Phase 1 (scope notes; no design decided)
- If school money pays for Phase 1 and it is $100,000 or less, the statute allows contracts without sealed bids. DCM still asks for 3 quotes on projects in its jurisdiction.
- Donated pads or labor still go through the district's process. Expect state review and inspection of the restroom and plumbing work.
- Plumbing work should be done by a licensed plumber unless the district confirms an exemption applies. This matches the scope sheet's "BY LICENSED PLUMBER / ENGINEER OF RECORD."
- The district's insurance carrier should weigh in before any volunteer work.
