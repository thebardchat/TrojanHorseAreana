## R-016.1 — Guests per suite

QUESTION: How many guests does a suite hold in a small or college venue?

ANSWER (one paragraph): **12 to 20.** S1: "Smaller suites will typically accommodate 12 guests comfortably"; its "5 Courtside Luxury Suites … can seat up to 15 people". S2 (Diddle Arena, WKU): "The suite lease also comes with 16 tickets". S3 (Sheldon ISD high-school stadium): "20 guest booth passes … give 20 ADULT VIPs access to one of two suites". The test-fit uses 12 / 16 / 20 (low / mid / high).

SOURCE: S1, S2, S3.

EDITION / SECTION: S1 Unique Features + Private Suites; S2 article text; S3 Press Box section.

CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM (three precedents agree on the range; none is a standard).

NEEDS ARCHITECT CONFIRMATION: YES (suite program is the owner's choice).

## R-016.2 — Suite size

QUESTION: How big is a suite (seating ledge or balcony, lounge, kitchenette, restroom)?

ANSWER (one paragraph): S2: Diddle Arena suites measure "400 square feet", with "leather seats, carpet, two televisions and private bathrooms for the suite level", and come with 16 tickets. That gives **25 SF per guest**, used for the test-fit. It covers the seating ledge and lounge. Restrooms are shared on the suite level (S2), so they are counted separately with Table 2902.1 fixtures. S4 cross-check: Texas Tech has "24 suites which measure 276 sq. ft. and corner suites measure 611 sq. ft." (no guest count given). S3's high-school suites have "lounge seating, sink, and a refrigerator" plus a shared kitchen area and restrooms. **Kitchenette/pantry: no cited size → left out** (suites assumed served from the concession stand). **Suite width** (for frontage and corridor): ASSUMED = 2 ledge rows × 18 in per seat (IBC 1004.6 seat-length basis) + 2 ft for walls. **Suite corridor:** ASSUMED at the IBC Table 1020.3 minimum width of 44 in.

SOURCE: S2, S3, S4; S5 (1004.6, Table 1020.3).

EDITION / SECTION: S2 (2002); S4 (current page); IBC 2021 1004.6, Table 1020.3.

CONFIDENCE: LOW-MEDIUM (one source gives both SF and guests).

NEEDS ARCHITECT CONFIRMATION: YES.

## R-016.3 — Do suites count toward occupant load and fixtures?

QUESTION: Do suite guests count toward the IBC occupant load, exits and restroom fixtures?

ANSWER (one paragraph): **Yes.** S5 1004.6: "For areas having fixed seats and aisles, the occupant load shall be determined by the number of fixed seats installed therein. The occupant load for areas in which fixed seating is not installed, such as waiting spaces, shall be determined in accordance with Section 1004.5 and added to the number of fixed seats." So a suite's code load = ledge seats + lounge area ÷ 15 net (Table 1004.5, "Unconcentrated (tables and chairs) 15 net"). This is higher than the guest count: at 25 SF/guest and 6 SF/seat, 16 guests → 16 + ceil(304 ÷ 15) = 37. The test-fit uses the code load for exits and Table 2902.1 fixtures, but counts only guests as spectators. Fixtures are sized per level; 2902.3.3 allows toilets one story away, so this is conservative (R-009). 1004.9 requires the occupant load to be posted in assembly rooms.

SOURCE: S5; S8.

EDITION / SECTION: IBC 2021 1004.5 (Table), 1004.6, 1004.9; Table 2902.1, 2902.3.3.

CONFIDENCE: HIGH for the rule / MEDIUM for the lounge split.

NEEDS ARCHITECT CONFIRMATION: YES (the building official sets the function of each space, 1004.5).

## R-016.4 — Accessibility in suites

QUESTION: What do the IBC and ADA require for wheelchair spaces in suites?

ANSWER (one paragraph): S6 1109.2.2.2: "In each luxury box, club box and suite within arenas, stadiums and grandstands, wheelchair spaces shall be provided in accordance with Table 1109.2.2.1". Table 1109.2.2.1 gives 4-25 seats → 1 space, so **each suite of 12-20 guests needs 1 wheelchair space** plus a companion seat (1109.2.3). S7 ADA 221.2.1.2 says the same; its advisory says the count "is to be calculated box by box and suite by suite". S6 1109.2.4: in multilevel assembly seating, "Wheelchair spaces shall be provided in each luxury box, club box and suite". S7 221.2.3 requires lines of sight and dispersion ("choices of seating locations and viewing angles that are substantially equivalent to, or better than" other spectators). 221.2.2 requires wheelchair spaces to be "an integral part of the seating plan". The suite level must therefore be on an accessible route: the elevator stops there (S6 1104.4; R-015). Bowl wheelchair spaces (Table 1109.2.2.1, 501-5,000 = 6 + 1 per 150 over 500): 1,940 bowl seats → 16.

SOURCE: S6, S7.

EDITION / SECTION: IBC 2021 1104.4, 1109.2.2.1, 1109.2.2.2, 1109.2.3, 1109.2.4; 2010 ADA Standards 221.2.1.1, 221.2.1.2, 221.2.2, 221.2.3.

CONFIDENCE: HIGH.

NEEDS ARCHITECT CONFIRMATION: YES (sightlines need a section).

## R-016.5 — Egress: suites at the back of Level 2 vs a suite level above the top row

QUESTION: Do suites change exits, stairs or the number of stories?

ANSWER (one paragraph): **Suites on Level 2** (back of the upper tier) add to Level 2's occupant load and floor area. In the test-fit they never fit on base inputs, because Level 2 is already about the size of the L1 ring under it. **A suite level above the top row** (Diddle precedent: "our suites are clear at the top of the arena", S2) is a third story. Effects: (1) exit access stairs lose the two-story open-stair exception (S5 1019.3 exception 1), so they become enclosed interior exit stairways, "not less than 1 hour where connecting less than four stories" (1023.2); (2) exits per story by Table 1006.3.3; stair capacity per story considered individually (1005.3.1), never reduced downstream (1005.4: "shall not be reduced along the path of egress travel"); (3) the elevator must serve the suite level (1104.4); (4) an elevator as accessible means of egress is still not required below 4 stories (1009.2.1). Not checked: allowable stories and height for the construction type (IBC ch. 5); whether suites could qualify as a mezzanine (IBC 505) instead of a story.

SOURCE: S2, S5, S6.

EDITION / SECTION: IBC 2021 1005.3.1, 1005.4, Table 1006.3.3, 1009.2.1, 1019.3, 1023.2, 1104.4.

CONFIDENCE: HIGH for the rules / LOW for the geometry (no section drawn).

NEEDS ARCHITECT CONFIRMATION: YES.
