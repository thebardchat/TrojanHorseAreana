## R-005.1 — Mat size: competition circle, safety area, minimum mat

QUESTION: What size must a high school wrestling mat be?

ANSWER (one paragraph): **Wrestling area = a circle at least 28 ft in diameter, surrounded by and secured to a safety mat area "approximately 5 feet wide."** S1, Rule 2-1-2: "The wrestling area of the mat shall be a circular area with a minimum of 28 feet in diameter. Surrounding and secured to the wrestling area of the mat shall be a safety mat area approximately 5 feet wide." NFHS does not state a "minimum mat dimension" as one number; **28 + 5 + 5 = 38 ft across is arithmetic from 2-1-2**, and it matches the "38 × 38 ft" high-school mat that manufacturers sell (S7, secondary). KHSAA (S5, citing an NFHS point of emphasis) says "approximately 5 feet" must **not** be read as allowing less than 5 ft. Markings (2-1-3, 2-1-4): 2-inch inbounds lines; a 10-ft center circle (made **optional** in 2024-25, S3); starting lines 1 inch wide, 3 ft long, 12 in apart outside-to-outside, joined by red and green 1-inch lines. The "42-ft mat" in the concept notes is a larger size manufacturers sell (S7: 42 × 42 ft, usually with a 32-ft circle, the NCAA size). It is allowed by NFHS ("minimum" 28 ft) but **not required**.

SOURCE: S1 (Rule 2-1-2, 2-1-3, 2-1-4); S3 (center circle optional); S5; S7 (secondary).

EDITION / SECTION: NFHS Rule 2, Section 1, Art. 2–4. Text from the 2014-15 edition; current edition **UNVERIFIED (paywalled)**.

CONFIDENCE: HIGH that the 28-ft minimum circle + ~5-ft safety area is the NFHS rule (old full text + 2023-24 excerpt + no change listed through 2026-27). MEDIUM that the current book words it identically.

NEEDS ARCHITECT CONFIRMATION: YES (and confirm the current edition).

## R-005.2 — Thickness, compression, material

QUESTION: What thickness and material does NFHS require?

ANSWER (one paragraph): S1, Rule 2-1-1: "The wrestling mat shall be of uniform thickness not more than 4 inches thick nor less than the thickness of a mat which has the shock-absorbing qualities of at least 1-inch PVC vinyl-covered foam. All mats that are in sections shall be secured together." That is the whole NFHS rule found. **No compression or impact-test number (e.g. ASTM F355 / F1081) appears in the NFHS text found.** Manufacturers (S7, secondary) advertise mats around 1-5/8 in thick and state they meet ASTM F1081 / F355; those standards are cited by NCAA and manufacturers, not by the NFHS text reviewed. S1 also says (Rule 2 preamble) that equipment safety determinations "are the responsibility of equipment manufacturers."

SOURCE: S1 (Rule 2-1-1); S7 (secondary).

EDITION / SECTION: NFHS Rule 2-1-1 (2014-15 text; current **UNVERIFIED**).

CONFIDENCE: HIGH on the 1-inch-PVC-foam equivalent / 4-inch maximum (rule text). The manufacturer thickness is marketing data, LOW as a requirement.

NEEDS ARCHITECT CONFIRMATION: NO for the rule; the coach/AD picks the product.

## R-005.3 — Mat-to-wall and mat-to-mat spacing; benches and scorer's table

QUESTION: How much clear space is needed around and between mats?

ANSWER (one paragraph): S1, Rule 2-1-5: "The mat area includes the wrestling mat and a space of at least 10 feet surrounding the mat, as well as the team benches and scorer's table **where facilities permit**. The team bench in dual meets should be at least 10 feet from the wrestling mat and 10 feet from the scorer's table." Rule 2-2-1: in dual meets, team personnel stay "at least 10 feet from the edge of the mat and the scorer's table, where facilities permit." Rule 2-3 (scorer's table): at least 10 ft from the mat edge and benches, where facilities permit. Rule 2-2-2: in tournaments, up to two team personnel may sit in chairs at the mat edge (corner restricted zone allowed). **NFHS gives no separate mat-to-mat number** in the text found. KHSAA (S5) says the 5-ft safety area between the circles of two adjoining mats "cannot be ignored" (so overlapping safety areas are not allowed) and **recommends about 10 ft from the out-of-bounds circle to walls or immovable objects, even padded walls.** Note "where facilities permit" makes the 10-ft space a target, not an absolute; for a new building, design to it.

SOURCE: S1 (Rules 2-1-5, 2-2-1, 2-2-2, 2-3); S5 (secondary).

EDITION / SECTION: NFHS Rule 2 Sections 1–3 (2014-15 text; current **UNVERIFIED**).

CONFIDENCE: HIGH on the 10-ft surrounding space (rule text). MEDIUM on mat-to-mat practice (state interpretation only).

NEEDS ARCHITECT CONFIRMATION: YES.

QUESTION: Does NFHS require padding walls, columns or other objects near the mat?

ANSWER (one paragraph): **No NFHS wrestling rule requiring padding of nearby obstructions was found** in the text reviewed (S1 Rule 2). ## R-005.5 — How many mats for dual meets and tournaments?

QUESTION: How many mats are typical?

ANSWER (one paragraph): **NFHS does not set a mat count.** A dual meet is one match at a time ("A series of matches, one in each of the NFHS weight classes, constitutes a dual meet," S1 Rule 1-2-1), so one mat is enough for a dual meet (KEYSTONE inference from the rule, not a stated count). Rule 2-1-5 refers to "suggested configuration" diagrams for benches and the scorer's table; those diagrams were not readable in the text copy and were not checked. Tournament mat counts are set by whoever runs the event. Context only: Michigan's 2025-26 district manual (S8) asks for at least 3 regulation mats (4 recommended) for individual districts and 1–2 for team districts. For Alabama, older AHSAA pages (dated, about 2013-14) showed state rounds on 6 mats and finals on 3 mats, and older Super Section programs listed "(6 Mats)" — **LOW confidence, dated**. The 2026-27 AHSAA handbook (S6) does not state mat counts.

SOURCE: S1 (Rule 1-2-1, 2-1-5); S8 (secondary); S6; older AHSAA pages (dated).

EDITION / SECTION: n/a.

CONFIDENCE: HIGH that NFHS sets no count; LOW on the Alabama tournament counts.

NEEDS ARCHITECT CONFIRMATION: NO. **NEEDS SHANE / COACH CONFIRMATION: YES** (what events the arena is meant to host).

## R-005.6 — Does AHSAA (Alabama) add anything?

QUESTION: Does the AHSAA add mat requirements beyond NFHS?

ANSWER (one paragraph): **No AHSAA-specific mat specification was found.** The 2026-27 AHSAA wrestling section (S6) says: "All matches will be conducted under National Federation rules," and for the state tournament, "The tournament will be governed by AHSAA and National Federation rules." Related facts from S6: Super Section tournaments Feb. 11-13, 2027 (Montgomery and Birmingham); "Any team that provides a mat to be used in the Super Section Tournament will not be required to pay an Entry fee"; **State Tournament Feb. 18–20, 2027, Huntsville — Von Braun Center.** Caution: a search summary attributed a "32–42 ft circle, 2-in hair-felt, 5 ft / 8 ft obstruction" rule to a state office; the underlying document (an Alaska athletics page) is quoting the **NCAA** rule, not AHSAA or NFHS. **Do not use it.**

## R-005.7 — ARITHMETIC ONLY: how many full mats fit in the Phase 2 22,000 SF main space?

**This is arithmetic from the sourced dimensions above. It is not a layout, not a design, and not a decision.** Room dimensions, clear span, seating, benches and scorer's-table positions are all TBD (phase2.yaml `clear_span: TBD`, `mat_size: TBD`). The 22,000 SF and "4 mats" come from the concept sheet / reference plan (phase2.yaml `spaces.arena`).

Inputs: NFHS minimum mat = 28-ft circle + 5-ft safety area each side = **38 ft square** (1,444 SF). Larger sold size = **42 ft square** (1,764 SF, S7). Clear space = **10 ft** (NFHS 2-1-5; KHSAA 10 ft to walls). KEYSTONE assumptions (not sourced rules): mats laid as squares; one 10-ft gap shared between adjacent mats (option A) or each mat keeps its own 10-ft ring (option B, 20 ft between mats).

| Case | Footprint | SF | Fits in 22,000 SF by area? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Concept check: 4 × 42-ft mats, mat surface only | 4 × 1,764 | 7,056 | yes ("≈ 7,000 SF" in concept notes checks out) |
| 2×2 grid, 38-ft mats, 10-ft perimeter + 10-ft shared gaps (A): side = 2×38 + 30 | 106 × 106 ft | 11,236 | yes |
| 2×2 grid, 42-ft mats, option A: side = 2×42 + 30 | 114 × 114 ft | 12,996 | yes |
| 2×2 grid, 38-ft mats, own 10-ft ring each (B): side = 2×38 + 40 | 116 × 116 ft | 13,456 | yes |
| 2×2 grid, 42-ft mats, option B: side = 2×42 + 40 | 124 × 124 ft | 15,376 | yes |
| 1×4 row, 42-ft mats, 10-ft gaps + 10-ft perimeter: (4×42 + 50) × (42 + 20) | 218 × 62 ft | 13,516 | yes |
| Area-only upper bound, own 10-ft ring per mat: 38-ft mat → 58 × 58 = 3,364 SF each | — | 22,000 / 3,364 = 6.5 | **6 mats** by area alone |
| Area-only upper bound, 42-ft mat → 62 × 62 = 3,844 SF each | — | 22,000 / 3,844 = 5.7 | **5 mats** by area alone |

Reading: by area alone, **4 full mats fit in 22,000 SF in every case above**, using 51–70% of the floor before benches, scorer's tables, aisles and seating. Whether they really fit depends on the room's actual length × width (a 22,000 SF room narrower than the grid side does not take a 2×2 grid), where seating sits (inside or outside the 22,000 SF), and bench/table placement (10 ft each per NFHS 2-1-5 / 2-3). The area-only bounds ignore geometry and are not achievable layouts as stated.

CONFIDENCE: arithmetic is exact; the inputs carry the confidence of R-005.1 and R-005.3.
