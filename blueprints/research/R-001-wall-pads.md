## R-001.1 — What do NFHS rules / NFHS guidance say about wall padding near mats?

QUESTION: Does NFHS require wall pads in a wrestling room, and what does it say about walls near mats?

ANSWER (one paragraph): **We could not confirm that the NFHS Wrestling Rules Book has any rule requiring wall pads in a practice room.** The rules book is sold, not free. The one third-party copy found returned HTTP 403. Two related items were verified:
- **Competition runoff.** NFHS Rule 2-1-2 sets a wrestling circle of at least 28 ft in diameter, with a safety mat area about 5 ft wide around it. Rule 2-1-5 adds a mat area of at least 10 ft around the mat "where facilities permit." Both are quoted in a Kentucky (KHSAA) interpretation of an NFHS point of emphasis. That interpretation adds: "Even if the wall is padded, there should be an approximate safety mat area of 10 feet from the out of bounds circle to the padded wall." This is about meets (competition), not practice rooms.
- **Practice-room guidance.** NFHS's own *High School Today* published an architect's article (Mark Beebe, AIA, Nov 8, 2022). It says perimeter walls "should be faced with a similar wrestling sports mat to around 6 feet high." It describes a renovated room with "2-inch wall padding up to 6 feet high," with doors and door hardware recessed from the wall surface. It also says walls should be flat and free of corners, pilasters, and bump-outs. **This is guidance, not a rule.**

Alabama high school wrestling runs under NFHS rules (AHSAA 2026-27 wrestling publication: "All matches will be conducted under National Federation rules").

SOURCE:

CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM on the guidance (~6 ft high, 2 in thick, recessed door hardware). LOW on whether any NFHS *rule* covers practice-room wall pads (not verified).

## R-001.2 — Typical pad height, coverage, and specs (thickness, foam type)

QUESTION: What are common wrestling/gym wall pad sizes and builds?

ANSWER (one paragraph): The sources agree on a common build: a **2 in thick panel, foam core laminated to a 7/16 in OSB backer, with a vinyl cover, about 6 ft tall.**
- Resilite (wrestling mat maker) sells 2 ft × 6 ft, 2 in panels in three versions:
  - **Guardian, 2 in:** cross-linked polyethylene core. **"Foam core not fire-rated."**
  - **Guardian XL, 2-1/2 in:** cross-linked polyethylene core. "Meets ASTM F2440." **"Foam core not fire-rated."**
  - **SafeGuard Plus, 2 in:** neoprene compound core. "Fire-retardant foam core (Meets ASTM E84 Standard)."
  All three use 14-oz fire-retardant vinyl. Install options: furring strips, slot-back on block walls, or aluminum Z-channel.
- Gill Athletics "FireSafe" pads: 2 in neoprene foam bonded to 7/16 in wood board, 19-oz vinyl. They claim "Entire assembly is ASTM E-84 Class 'A' fire rated and tested in accordance with NFPA 286."
- A Porter Athletic white paper cites ASTM F2440 (indoor wall/feature padding). It says pads are typically installed no more than 4 in above the floor and up to 6 ft tall, and that F2440 sets minimum shock absorption.

**These are manufacturer (secondary) sources. KEYSTONE did not read ASTM F2440 itself.**

## R-001.3 — Fire performance required for wall pads on school interior walls

QUESTION: What fire performance does code require for foam wall pads in a public school wrestling room in Alabama?

ANSWER (one paragraph): **Code treats wall pads as interior finish, and foam pads need a full-panel fire test, not just component ratings.**
- **Wall pads count as interior finish.** IBC 2021 §806.2: "Fixed or movable walls and partitions, paneling, wall pads, and crash pads … shall be considered interior finish, shall comply with Section 803, and shall not be considered decorative materials or furnishings."
- **The room-level minimum is Class C.** Table 803.13 requires Class C (ASTM E84 flame spread 76–200, smoke-developed 0–450, §803.1.2) for "rooms and enclosed spaces" in Groups B/E/M/R-1 and A-3/A-4/A-5, sprinklered or not.
- **But foam pads need a large-scale assembly test, so Class C alone isn't enough.** §803.4: "Foam plastics shall not be used as interior finish except as provided in Section 2603.9. This section shall apply both to exposed foam plastics and to foam plastics used in conjunction with a textile or vinyl facing or cover."
- §2603.9 allows foam only "where specifically approved based on large-scale tests such as … NFPA 286 (with the acceptance criteria of Section 803.1.1.1), FM 4880, UL 1040 or UL 1715." The test must be on "the finished manufactured foam plastic assembly in the maximum thickness intended for use," including seams and joints.
- NFPA 286 acceptance criteria are in §803.1.1.1 (no flame to the ceiling at 40 kW, no flashover, a peak heat release limit, a total smoke limit). A pass also counts as Class A (§803.1.1).
- **Practical result:** specify a **complete wall pad assembly tested to NFPA 286 (or another large-scale test the code official approves)**, not pads whose vinyl or foam carries its own E84 rating. The Porter white paper reaches the same conclusion.
- **Alabama:** DCM's State Building Code is the **2021 IBC** (adopted July 1, 2022, amended March 17, 2025 with some 2024 sections; none of the amendments touch Chapter 8). It also adopts the 2021 IEBC and 2021 IFC. It applies to "any and all work … on all public and private kindergarten through twelfth grade (K-12) schools."

## R-001.4 — Does remodel work trigger these rules, and does any IFC threshold change things?

QUESTION: For an existing school room, do the new-construction interior finish rules apply to new pads?

ANSWER (one paragraph): **We couldn't fully confirm this.**
- Alabama adopts the 2021 IEBC for existing buildings (DCM page). The specific IEBC alteration section that sends new interior finish to IBC Chapter 8 was not retrieved.
- The fire code has its own wall-pad section (Minnesota's code, based on the IFC, §807.2.1). It treats pads as interior finish only when they cover **more than 10% of the wall or ceiling area**. In existing sprinklered buildings that becomes **more than 20%**.
- A wrestling room padded about 6 ft high on its walls will very likely exceed either threshold, so the interior finish rules (R-001.3) should be assumed to apply.
- The IFC section number and threshold seen here are from Minnesota's fire code (a state version), not Alabama's adopted 2021 IFC.

## Conflicts / flags

- **Manufacturer "meets ASTM E84" vs IBC §803.4.** Resilite's fire-retardant option says only "meets ASTM E84." Its standard Guardian lines say "foam core not fire-rated." The IBC (§803.4 → §2603.9) requires a large-scale test of the finished assembly (e.g., NFPA 286) for foam interior finish. **An E84-only or unrated pad may not pass plan review.** Gill's FireSafe claims an NFPA 286 assembly test. Flag for product selection (D-003).
- **IBC vs IFC threshold.** IBC §806.2 has no percentage threshold. The IFC-based §807.2.1 seen (Minnesota) applies above 10% / 20%. The difference is unlikely to matter here (full-wall pads), but it's noted.
- **Competition runoff vs practice room.** The 10 ft "to the padded wall" recommendation (KHSAA/NFHS) is for meets. A practice-room remodel isn't bound by it, but it limits whether the room could ever host a sanctioned match. Don't apply it to Phase 1 without Shane's call.

## What this means for Phase 1 (scope notes; no design decided)

- W1 pads should be specified as a **complete assembly tested to NFPA 286 (or approved equal)**. Budget and donation discussions must assume a fire-rated product, not the cheapest unrated pad.
- Outlets, switches, thermostats, and doors on padded walls need cutouts or relocation. The NFHS article recommends recessed doors and hardware. That ties to checklist item 6 and to P1-A-201.
- Pad height: guidance points to about 6 ft. Final height stays **TBD (D-003)**.
