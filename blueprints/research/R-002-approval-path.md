## R-002.1 — Does state review (Alabama DCM) apply to alteration work at a public K-12 school?

QUESTION: Does state plan review or inspection apply to remodel work (wall pads, plumbing repair, restroom split) inside an existing Madison County public high school?

ANSWER (one paragraph): **DCM's published rules say yes: DCM reviews and inspects work on public K-12 schools, at any cost and from any funding source.**
- DCM's page for fully locally-funded public K-12 projects says: "Plans and specifications of all K-12 projects must be submitted to DCM for review, regardless of estimated construction cost. All public K-12 projects require a DCM pre-construction conference and DCM inspections, regardless of estimated construction cost."
- The same page defines "fully locally-funded" as money from the local owner, "including but not limited to federal and local funds, grants and donations." So **donated money does not take a project out of DCM's process.**
- The DCM Manual of Procedures (Ch. 1, March 2026) lists DCM's jurisdiction as "any new construction, addition, renovation, modification of mechanical, electrical, plumbing systems, sitework and generally any and all work … on public (including charter) and private schools regardless of funding sources, including all K-12."
- DCM fees:
  - Projects of $750,000 or less owe **no** DCM plan-review or permit fees, but "are still subject to DCM plan reviews" and inspections.
  - DCM's Plan Review Comment Letter on the final submittal is needed before bids.
  - Small projects of limited scope (e.g. "minor alterations") skip the schematic and preliminary submittals (Manual Ch. 3).
- Plans must come from a design professional. DCM's self-performance page says: "Any construction plans and specifications … must be issued by a design professional and must be submitted to DCM for review and approval."
- DCM's pages for small and owner-performed projects start with **"Step 1: Project Verification."** The owner contacts DCM Technical Staff to confirm "whether or not a project requires review and/or administration … by DCM." **So DCM itself decides, case by case, whether a given small scope needs its review.** No source said whether wall pads alone (a finish item) need DCM review.
- Contracts: on fully locally-funded K-12 projects, the owner–architect agreements and construction contracts go to the **State Department of Education (SDE) School Architect's Office** for review. DCM gets a copy.

NEEDS ARCHITECT CONFIRMATION: YES. Also **NEEDS DISTRICT CONFIRMATION**: the district (Operations & Facilities) or its architect should make the DCM "project verification" call for the actual Phase 1 scope.

## R-002.2 — Who inside Madison County Schools approves this kind of work?

QUESTION: What is the approval path inside Madison County Schools (MCSS) for improvement work on a school campus, especially work helped by outside groups or donors?

ANSWER (one paragraph): **The one MCSS document found that covers this is the district's *Booster & Support Organizations Guidelines*. It describes a path of principal → Board of Education → state review, with a Board employee in charge.**
- Under "SELF-WORK PROJECTS" it says:
  - "Any building or building/land improvement on a school district campus should be approved by the Board of Education."
  - "A Board employee should be in charge of the project."
  - "Project must comply with Building Commission requirements":
    - "Full professional design team required"
    - "Plan review and approval required"
    - "Architect required to perform inspections"
    - "Building Commission will conduct required inspections including final inspection"
    - "Compliance with Public Works Law or Competitive Bid Law will be applicable in most situations depending on the funding sources"
  - "All projects should follow the same process regardless of funding."
- On the principal's role, the same guidelines say the principal or designee should "approve any activity of the Club that affects the school." Booster clubs may not "spend money for the school's benefit without the knowledge and approval of the principal or designee."
- The MCSS website lists an **Operations & Facilities** department (Chief of Operations; Director of Operations) at 1275 Jordan Road, Huntsville. Its stated mission is "to construct and maintain quality facilities." This is the district facilities office for the scope sheet's "district facilities" step.
- The MCSS Board Policy manual is hosted on Simbli. It did not load as readable text, so **no MCSS board policy on facilities, donations, or volunteers was read.**
- Path the sources support (draft; needs district confirmation):
  1. Principal
  2. MCSS Operations & Facilities
  3. Madison County Board of Education approval for building/land improvements
  4. State review and inspection (DCM), plus SDE School Architect review of contracts if there is a construction contract

## Conflicts / flags
- **Outdated agency name:** the MCSS guidelines say "Alabama Building Commission." DCM's Manual (Ch. 1 §B) says Act 2015-435 abolished the Building Commission effective October 1, 2015, and its powers moved to DCM. Read "Building Commission" in the district document as DCM.
- **Small-project wording:** DCM's K-12 page says every K-12 project is reviewed "regardless of estimated construction cost." DCM's small-project and self-performance pages say to first verify "whether or not a project requires review." Both are DCM's own pages. The district should get DCM's answer for this scope.

## What this means for Phase 1 (scope notes; no design decided)
- Expect a **design professional** (architect/engineer) on the remodel if DCM review applies, which is likely for the restroom split and plumbing.
- Expect **DCM inspections** and a final inspection on public K-12 work, even with donated money.
- The scope sheet's "who approves" order (principal → district facilities → others) matches the sources. "Others" likely includes the **Board of Education** and **state review (DCM)**. The district should confirm.
